What changed from the first model

  • The “turret” was two different things. The 61.6 mm width was the Ø59 movable disc, not the enclosure. The enclosure is a 44 mm square cage: a solid 19 mm block with four 6.2 mm corner posts rising to 41.4 mm, chamfered on the vertical edges, with open windows on every face.
  • Both joints share one actuator stack (housing Ø39.5, harmonic-drive step Ø36.5, recess Ø29, bore Ø12.3, 18.1 mm long) plus a Ø58 four-screw flange behind it. At the shoulder the flange is fixed to the base; at the elbow it carries the forearm.
  • Link ends carry a six-screw hub pattern (R10.5, screws at link angle ± 30°) and the tip has a Ø12.3 bore with six R10.6 aux holes. Slots are 5.8 mm wide, not 5.1.
  • Link length L1 = L2 = 102.0 mm (was 99.4 / 103.8 at a different scale). Shoulder axis is higher: tilt-corrected, the camera looks down 6.5°, so near-camera features read low.
  • Depth stack is now evidence-based (frame 2 horizontal axis, cross-checked by the frame 1 / frame 4 parallax), and it differs from the old assumption: the connector beam is on the camera side of both actuators and the forearm sits behind the elbow actuator.

Read this before trusting a number

  • Built from annotation names and coordinates, not from the frame images. Where a polygon’s meaning was ambiguous I chose the reading that agreed with other evidence and listed it below. Check the flagged parts against the frames.
  • Assumed: the optical plate and counterbores (carried over), the yaw actuator, mount X-widths (41 / 40), the upright’s Y-extent and the simplified top, and the 5.7 mm beam standoff at the elbow.
  • Unresolved: a 2.5 mm gap between the spacer top and the disc underside; the elbow mount’s attachment (modelled on the forearm); the frame-3 pose (elbow 63.9 mm above the shoulder gives an upper arm of 39° or 141°, so it is left out).
  • One scale for every frame. The base enclosure’s front face measures 38.5–40.5 mm across frames 1–4, a ≈5 % spread, so treat off-plane features as ±2–3 %.
